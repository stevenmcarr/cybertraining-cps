NSF CyberTraining: Pilot · Collaborative Research

Concurrent Computing Curricula for Cyber-Physical Systems

Learning modules and open tools that teach engineers to reason about concurrency, timing, and fault tolerance in the software that runs cars, aircraft, and robots.

Western Michigan University & Michigan Technological University August 2026 – July 2028
About the project Learning modules

Why this project

Automobiles, aircraft, and other safety-critical systems depend on software that performs many tasks at once. Small timing or coordination mistakes such as race conditions, deadlocks, and stale data can go unnoticed in testing and later cause unsafe behavior.

The engineers who build these systems are often trained in mechanics and electronics rather than concurrent programming. This project develops a modular curriculum and open educational tools that give them hands-on experience with concurrent and distributed computing, grounded in the platforms they use: POSIX threads, FreeRTOS, and ROS 2.
