People

Team

A collaboration between Western Michigan University, the lead institution, and Michigan Technological University, combining expertise in concurrent systems education, visualization, and cyber-physical systems applications.

Lead PI · Western Michigan University

Overall project management, including budget oversight, timeline management, and communication across institutions. Oversees development of the CEDL compiler and interpreter, and supervises the addition of security material to the modules.

PI · Michigan Technological University

Every module is reviewed within the project team before classroom deployment. Investigators meet biweekly by video conference, hold monthly curriculum coordination calls, file annual progress reports, and share Git repositories that both institutions own.

Mentoring cyberinfrastructure professionals

Individual development plans

The PI meets weekly with each student. At the first meeting the student receives an Individual Development Plan form and a clear statement of their responsibilities; at the second, student and PI review the completed plan and identify ways to meet the student's goals through the project and through university resources.

The plan guides advising on research, publication, and technical presentation. Students headed for academic careers work with the PI and the department to find supervised teaching experience before graduation. Late in the project year, students receive this proposal and other examples, and may help write follow-up proposals.

Industry mentorship

The computer science, mechanical and aerospace engineering, and electrical engineering departments at WMU maintain active industrial advisory boards of senior engineers and technical leaders from automotive, aerospace, and cybersecurity organizations. Students are paired with board members whose expertise matches their work, meet them at annual board meetings, and present their work for feedback.

WMU's Custer Workplace Series brings industry speakers to campus. The project will host speakers on cyber-physical systems and concurrency: automotive engineers on real-world race conditions in vehicle software, and aviation engineers on fault tolerance and timing-critical avionics. Post-seminar discussions with faculty mentors connect those insights back to the curriculum and tools.
